## Visual rules — must match
- **Canvas:** Nearly black/navy-charcoal background, subtly warm/dark gradient. Thin crisp antique-gold frames, corner ornaments, narrow gilded section separators. Never cream/pale parchment backgrounds. A little cream is allowed as lettering and highlights, not as the sheet canvas.
- **Rendering:** Richly detailed modern-retro game pixel-art aesthetic; dense intentional pixel clusters with visibly crisp/pixel-sharp edges, strong dark contours, saturated but controlled earth/metal/gem colors, layered warm highlights and deep shadows. Do not replace with flat 8-bit sprites, rounded pastel cartoon, cel-shaded flat illustration, or soft low-detail RPG icon figures.
- **Faces:** The **specific elegant anime-influenced adult faces** from images 1 and 7: expressive almond eyes, confident/serious or gentle expressions as shown, clean small noses and mouths, long flowing or braided hair, distinctive upright pointed equine ears. Do not substitute generic large-faced chibi/cutesy smiles or softened children's-game proportions.
- **Builds:** Preserve pronounced and highly stylized feminine upper-body proportions and costumes from the approved reference; strong defined waist/torso and substantial, believable pony-horse lower body. The equine body is compact in LENGTH for Knomira's space, **not a tiny cartoon block**, with strong rounded haunches, readable four legs/hooves, complex fur and flowing tails. Female centaurs should match the ORIGINAL approved designs even when drawing anatomy.
- **Breed differentiation:** Standard = balanced compact; Clydesdale/Draftkin = visibly broad, powerfully muscled, massive body and dense fetlock feathering; Milkmaid/Pasturekin = rounded pastoral build and pinto/dappled coats, farm accessories; Noble Courser/Paladin = slender, taller regal white/silver body and elegant blue/white/gold ceremonial armor; Mustang/Stepperunner = athletic rugged, chestnut/dark coat, nomadic tack/feathers/scarf. Maintain breed-specific upper-body and horse-body proportions as drawn; don't just recolor or swap clothes.
- **Gear:** Detailed layered leather belts, buckles, pouches, textiles, ornate metal pauldrons, blue/gold paladin plate, class props; believable material detail at matching pixel density.
- **Composition:** Gold/cream fantasy serif headings, large role sprites, portrait close-ups, class/sex labels, compact expressions, body-part panels, coats/hair/tails/accessories; enough breathing room that the primary characters remain big and legible. Panels are placed on charcoal background, NOT light parchment.
- **Viewpoint:** The full-body adult fantasy proportions and 3/4 angle of originals, not squat toy-like horse figurines. Turnarounds and health overlays must use the same visual language.
- **Expressions:** Dedicated panels; preserve face structure and approved eye style across neutral, focused, happy, irritated, injured etc. Draw different expressions on the SAME face rather than replacing the face design.
- **Anatomy/health-zone additions:** Add diagrams *inside the exact approved visual language*. Split human torso, left/right arms, equine barrel, forelegs/hindlegs, hooves, tail, head/neck, as appropriate. Diagram colors can be used for overlays but should not become the style of the base character; health mapping is a separate layer. Body part closeups must inherit the same texture, silhouette and colors.

## Exclusions: known rejected drift
After the original seven, multiple revisions used parchment-white backgrounds, softened simplified pixel characters, less dimensional/less mature faces, flatter 16-bit icons, changed costume designs and scaled-down toy-like pony bodies. The user explicitly rejected this drift. **Do not treat those images as baseline even if they have health-zone overlays.** The initial standalone female Centaur anatomy board was accepted for the *idea and functionality* of anatomy labels/overlays, not as authority to replace the original character visual direction.

## Before generating a new image
1. Inspect original #1 and #7 for faces, anatomy, costumes and pixel density; #2–#6 for breed anatomy, classes, palettes and panel layout.
2. Use original art images as visual reference inputs whenever supported. Text alone is less reliable.
3. Do NOT introduce a new visual style while adding technical documentation. Match existing characters exactly and add panels around them.
4. Check finished output: dark charcoal gold-trimmed frame? approved adult face? correct proportions? correct breed? dense outlines/shading? no new pale parchment/soft-cartoon look? If any are wrong, **do not present it as the new official baseline**.
